Bus → train uses the first scheduled train allowing your walk. Train → bus uses the closest scheduled first-visit bus within your chosen time window, including gaps too short to walk. Matching uses schedules before actual delays; a later service never hides a miss. For bus → train, a bus departure can prove a connection conservatively: the bus arrived no later than it departed. A short gap using that bound stays unknown. For train → bus, a recorded arrival is needed to confirm the maximum wait; a departure alone can only prove the wait was already too long. Missing departure times for the service you want to board stay unknown. When the train has no station arrival or departure report, a recorded bus departure enables a timetable-based conclusion for bus → train. It uses recorded arrival where available, otherwise departure as the latest possible arrival, and allows your walking time. It assumes trains do not leave early; likely misses can change if the train is delayed or the bus arrived earlier. These conclusions stay outside the reported transfer rate. GPS adds separate likely-possible / likely-missed estimates when stop reports are missing. It requires multiple readings within 35 metres of the bus stop showing a dwell, and uses timing ranges rather than exact arrivals. Positions near the station do not prove doors opened. When station reports are missing, captured station predictions are kept until later stop progress confirms the vehicle passed the station. Nearby-stop delay projections are also shown as estimates with at least one minute of working uncertainty. Estimates are excluded from the reported transfer rate. Pending means the intended service has not yet supplied departure evidence. Bounds are labelled explicitly; they are not exact arrivals. Both bus visits require two distinct scheduled station sequences. This estimates opportunity from recorded times; it does not prove someone boarded.

How performance is measured & settings

Each observation is one reported arrival or departure at a stop, counted once per trip instance. Percentages describe measured stop events, not the share of whole trips that ran on time. Future predictions, skipped stops and stale updates are excluded. Events with no reported delay are stored as unknown and excluded from percentages. Cancellations are counted separately.

On time: between 1 minute early and 5 minutes late, inclusive. This is your analysis window, not an official AT target.

History is kept for three calendar months. Daily cleanup removes older stop events, cancellations and station timetables by Auckland service date, including paused routes. Export any history you want to keep longer.

Collection starts when the server or cloud schedule runs. Missing observations do not imply a service was on time or cancelled. Twenty-second cloud sampling may miss some intermediate stops; history cannot be backfilled from the live feed.
